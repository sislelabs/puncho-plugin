# Events, courses and programmes for young people

Read this for a brand that sells something people sign up for: an event, a
course, a programme, especially to a young audience. It replaces the main
skill's defaults where they disagree. The worked examples are real copy from a
free tech event for teenagers in a Bulgarian town, and they belong to that
brand: learn from the reasons, never reuse the lines.

## What to push against, for this category

1. **A status the reader does not have yet.** The strongest for young
   audiences: who they become, not what they lack.
2. **A named enemy.** A price, an institution, a waiting list, the people who
   make you wait for permission.
3. **A feeling they already had**, named precisely enough to feel spied on.
4. **A belief they hold that is wrong**, contradicted flatly.
5. **A specific number or fact**, only with something real behind it.

## Go and find out what physically happens

The brief said "hackathon and conference, registrations, don't invent
anything". Fifteen hooks were written off it. Research then found the event is
two parts, that the conference exists so people with no technical knowledge can
take part, and that the hack is 36 hours to build a working prototype with
mentors, a team and AI. That last fact was worth more than every line written
without it.

## The substitution test, worked

| Hook | Swap | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Идваш с идея. Тръгваш си с проект." | Any hackathon anywhere | **Dead.** True of the format, not this event. |
| "Миналата година беше първото издание." | Any second-year anything | **Dead.** A fact, not a hook. |
| "Едно време дори и тези са били просто деца с идеи" | No competitor is making this argument | **Alive.** Reframes the reader's distance from people they admire. |

## The paraphrase test, worked

| Hook | Paraphrase | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Безплатно е. Останалото не е." | "It's free, but the rest of… what?" | **Fake.** Nothing survives the paraphrase, and it tells the reader there is a hidden cost when the event is free. |
| "В Пазарджик никога нищо не се случва. Освен сега." | "There is finally something happening here." | **Real,** but it insults the brand's own town. |
| "Второто издание. Първото го изпусна." | "This is the second one; you missed the first." | **Real,** and deficit-framed (below). |

## Symmetry, worked

"Домашното го предаваш. Проекта го показваш." says something real and was
rejected on sight. Symmetry sounds composed, and composed sounds like a slogan
on a school corridor wall: to a teenager, the register of the institution the
brand is trying not to be.

## The reader is never the antagonist, worked

Rejected as a batch, "really lame and not in our voice", because every line was
built on what the reader lacks:

| Deficit | Potential |
|---|---|
| "Хакатон за хора, които не знаят какво е хакатон." | "Не ти трябва диплома, за да направиш нещо, което работи." |
| "Второто издание. Първото го изпусна." | "Второто издание. Този път си вътре." |
| "Знаеш синтаксиса. Не си правил проект." | "Никой няма да ти даде разрешение." |

A brand whose tone notes say "talk like a peer, not like a school" has told you
that school, institutions and gatekeepers are fair targets. Aim there.

## Organic slang in paid

The brand says "тарикат" in organic; in paid it is off-limits. The register it
signals (peer, cheeky, unofficial) is what carries across, not the word.

## The audience's word

Where a language has a formal term and a spoken one, the spoken one is almost
always right: "CV", not "автобиография". The formal word is the register of
the school and the form you fill in.
