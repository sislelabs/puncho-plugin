# Rejected copy, and why

Real output from this pipeline, rejected by the client on sight. Every line was
produced by a model that had just been told to be specific and avoid clichés —
which is the point. The bans are not sufficient; the tests are.

The copy comes from several brands, described here and not named. The lines belong
to their brand, so never reuse one. The reasons apply to every brand.

Read these before drafting.

---

# An organic food brand (Bulgaria)

**"Млякото ни има стопанин. Казва се Николай."** and **"През май млякото ни е
по-жълто. Заради глухарчетата."**: rejected by the client as "strange".

Both are true, researched and specific, and both lead a cold ad with the brand
talking about itself. To a stranger, a farmer's name and a pasture fact are
answers to a question nobody asked yet. "Стопанин" also makes the milk sound
owned. It is a word written for effect, not said.

The facts were good. The mistake was where they went. The dandelion fact needs
two "so what?"s before it reaches the reader: it tastes different in May, and
the milk in your fridge doesn't. The farmer is proof for the primary text.

The next five drafts, one per product, went the other way and fell into the
crowded claim. Four of six were ingredient-purity lines ("маслото е масло",
"Лешници от България. И толкова."), in the one place the competitor research
had just said every rival was standing. The worked example in
`categories/food.md` goes through them.

# A free hackathon for teenagers (a Bulgarian town)

---

**"Идваш с идея. Тръгваш си с проект."** — EDUCATIONAL / DEMONSTRATION

Reads well. Balanced, concrete, no banned words. Fails the substitution test
instantly: it is true of every hackathon that has ever run. It describes the
format of the event rather than this event, and nothing is pushed against.

---

**"Миналата година беше първото издание."** — SOCIAL_PROOF / PATTERN_INTERRUPT

A fact presented as a hook. There is no tension, no status, no antagonist, and
nothing for the reader to do with the information. Facts support hooks; they are
not hooks.

---

**"Знаеш синтаксиса. Не си правил проект."** — PROBLEM_AGITATE / RELATABLE_PAIN

The closest of the batch to working — it names a real gap. It fails on voice: the
tone is a teacher diagnosing you. The brand's own organic talks like a peer
taking the piss, and a peer would say this differently and more cheaply.

---

**"Безплатно. В Пазарджик."** — OFFER_LED / BOLD_CLAIM

Two true facts, no tension. "Free" is only a hook against an expectation of
paying, and nothing in the line sets that expectation up. The board note said to
lead with free — leading with it is not the same as making it land.

---

**"Кой от твоя клас вече се е записал?"** — CURIOSITY_GAP / QUESTION

Implies social proof that does not exist yet. It also asks a question the reader
cannot answer, which reads as a prompt to do homework rather than curiosity.

---

## The pattern

All five are *competent*. None is *specific to this brand*. They were written
from the brief while the reference material sat unread and untagged — the
references were classified only afterwards, and by then the copy had already been
written from the category average.

This is the failure mode to watch for in yourself: fluent, clean, on-brief copy
that any competitor could run unchanged.

---

**"Безплатно е. Останалото не е."** — OFFER_LED / PATTERN_INTERRUPT

Rejected by the client as "fake", and correctly. It passes the substitution test
and it has an antagonist, which is why it survived two rounds of editing — but
"the rest" refers to nothing. The two-beat reversal supplies the *feeling* of a
point while the sentence makes none.

It is also false in a costly direction: the only concrete thing a reader takes
from it is that something here is not free. The event is free, and the price
objection is the one this campaign most needs to remove.

The lesson is the paraphrase test. Say the line flat, to a person, without the
rhythm. If you cannot finish the sentence, the rhythm was doing all the work.

## Round three: the deficit pattern

Rejected as a batch — "really lame and not in our voice":

- "Мама пита кога ще направиш нещо с този компютър."
- "Безплатно. Не ти трябва опит. Трябва да се запишеш."
- "В Пазарджик никога нищо не се случва. Освен сега."
- "Второто издание. Първото го изпусна."
- "Хакатон за хора, които не знаят какво е хакатон."

Each passes the substitution test and the paraphrase test. They fail together,
for one reason: **every one is built on what the reader lacks.** No project, no
experience, missed the first edition, does not know what a hackathon is, a parent
who has noticed. The reader loses in all five.

The brand's own voice runs the other way. "Хакатонът, който ще те направи
тарикат" promises a status. Musk and Bezos under "едно време дори и тези са били
просто деца с идеи" tells the reader they are already the same kind of person as
people they admire. Audacious, proud, and the reader wins.

Two further tells in that batch:

- "В Пазарджик никога нищо не се случва" insults the brand's own town. The brand
  is proud of being in Pazardzhik; the note said it is "от малкото неща в
  Пазарджик", which is a boast, not an apology.
- "Хакатон за хора, които не знаят какво е хакатон" defines the event downward.
  Lowering the barrier is a good instinct; sounding remedial is not the way.

The cause was a rule in this skill. "Every hook needs something to push against"
made the reader the nearest antagonist three times running. The fix is the
section "The reader is never the antagonist".

# Captions, and one line said four times (four product brands)

Found reading the words on forty-five designed ads for an iced coffee, a
protein topping, a snack maker and a shoe brand, in Bulgarian and English. The
pictures were good, and the words were the reason the ads read as generic.

## Headlines that label the picture

- "3 AM"
- "18 hours."
- "Still out in the snow."
- "Sneakers for the first snow."
- "Вземи ги и тръгвай."

Each names a time, a number, a weather or the product, and claims nothing.
Over its photograph each looks finished; covered, each says nothing a reader
could act on. "18 hours" is the brew time, a real fact with no "so what":
what does eighteen hours do that ten minutes does not, for the reader? That
sentence was the hook, and nobody wrote it.

## The best line was in the body

- Headline: "Answer all of them. It's still cold." Body text's first line:
  "Hot coffee goes cold while you answer one email."
- Headline: "Поне 20 g протеин в обяда, без да печеш още пилешко". Caption on
  the same ad: "почти колкото протеина в 100 g пилешко филе".

In both, the body or the caption holds the line a reader would stop for: a
moment everyone has had, a comparison with a food everyone knows. The headline
above it was a summary. Pass 3 of the skill, promote the strongest line, is
the fix.

## One fact, every place, every ad

- "Handles snow and wet weather": the store's product line, in all thirteen
  concepts of one brief, in the kicker, the body text and the badge, often
  twice on one ad.
- "Cold brew steeped 18 hours, real oat milk, 4 g sugar": in nearly every ad of
  another brand, as kicker, body text and Meta headline at once.
- Four hooks on one brief that were one line: "Christmas market at noon.
  Dinner at eight." with "One pair." or "Same pair." added or left off.
- A Meta headline that repeats the image's headline on forty of a hundred and
  seventeen concepts.

None of it is false, and none of it trips the tension warning: the tensions
differed while the words did not. A reader scrolling past three of these ads
has seen one ad three times. Each place on an ad has its own job (the skill's
"Four places for words, four jobs"), and a fact lives in the one place where
it makes the hook true.

# A stock idea: "it's mine, hands off" (three brands, one day)

Written in every concept run for a day, on brands that share nothing:

- an iced coffee: "Who took my oat latte?", "Whoever took it, get your own.",
  "Whose is this one?", "Not yours. I'm serious.", "Hands off, this one's
  mine." (one office-fridge theft, five ways, across four batches);
- a chocolate snack, in Bulgarian: "Розовите са мои." over a chat that ends
  "Не.";
- relabelled by the writer, when refused, as a "shared space", then "home,
  morning", then "family", the same words each time.

Each one alone is a real moment with real tension, which is why it keeps
coming back: it is the model's nearest idea for "a product people want",
for any product. That is what makes it a stock idea. On a brand's board it is
used once at most, and only when it says something only this product could
(why this one is the one people take). A second, in any words, on any brief, is
the same ad. When it comes to mind first, take it as the sign to look for the
idea that belongs to this product: what it does, where it is used, what it
replaces.
