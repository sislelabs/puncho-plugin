# Five strong statics, in words

**These show a level, never a layout to repeat.** Each is here to show how
good a static gets and why, so that "done" means this and not "no findings".
A design that could be mistaken for one of them, or that reuses its
composition, its palette or its device, is a copy, and a copy fails however
well it is built. Take the principle named under each, and build it from the
concept's own idea, the brand's own colours and the brand's own pictures.

Every design names, in its finish note, which principle it took from this
file and how it differs from the example it came from. "None of them
applied" is an answer too, with a reason.

The words are described by their job, never quoted: an example's lines would
be borrowed otherwise. The palette is given by role (accent, ink, paper,
secondary), because the colours themselves are always the brand's.

## 1. A spread, mid-scoop — `FOOD_IN_HAND`, `CORNER_PACK`

- **Picture:** a photograph, three quarters of the frame. A knife lifting a
  thick curl of the spread off an open jar, a slice of toast under it with one
  corner bitten. Side light from the left, so the swirl in the spread shows
  its ridges and gloss. The wall behind is the pack's warm signature colour,
  the counter a darker shade of it.
- **Layout:** the headline across the top fifth, on the clean wall the
  photograph was made with. The real jar, flat and front-on, in the lower
  right corner at about a fifth of the width, with a soft drop shadow, like a
  sticker. The logo is on the jar; nothing else signs it.
- **Type:** a heavy display face, three short lines, tight leading, ink on the
  signature colour. One supporting line under it at a third of its size: the
  product's name and what is in it. Nothing else on the image.
- **Palette roles:** paper is the wall (the pack's signature), ink the type,
  accent unused: the food is the colour.
- **Why it works:** one thing to look at, and it is the food doing what the
  reader wants it to do. The pack is not pretending to be in the room, so
  nothing looks pasted. The headline is a confession, and the bitten toast is
  its proof.
- **What to take:** the appetite is in motion, and the pack signs it from a
  corner instead of competing with it.
- **Do not take:** the knife and toast, the warm wall, the top-fifth headline,
  the lower-right pack.

## 2. One number on a field — `DATA_HERO`, `GRAPHIC_FIELD`

- **Picture:** the real bottle of a face serum, front-on, a little under half
  the frame's height, standing on a field of its own pale label colour with
  only a contact shadow. Beside it, one generated drop of the serum, cut out,
  catching the light.
- **Layout:** a split. The number fills the left half, cropped slightly by
  the frame's left edge; the bottle and the drop stand in the right half,
  their base on the same line as the number's foot.
- **Type:** the number at about a third of the frame's height, in the
  brand's display face. Under it one short line saying what the number
  counts (how many ingredients it has, in the brand's own claim from the
  label). The headline is the number; there is no second headline.
- **Palette roles:** paper is the label colour, ink the number, accent one
  small mark on the line under it. Secondary unused.
- **Why it works:** a single checkable fact, so specific that it argues by
  itself, and nothing on the frame competes with it. The field is the
  pack's own colour, so the ad reads as the product before a word is read.
- **What to take:** one true fact made into the picture, with the product
  close enough to be the proof.
- **Do not take:** the split, the drop, the left-cropped number, the pale
  field.

## 3. A can in the reader's hand — `HEADLINE_PRODUCT`, `IN_SCENE` (brand selfie)

- **Picture:** a cold can of canned coffee held in a hand, seen from the
  holder's own eyes, on a commuter train: the knee and the seat fabric below,
  the window with a blurred morning outside to the right. The real can was
  placed first, upright, where the grip would be, and `build_scene` made the
  hand and the carriage round it; condensation on the can, low sun through
  the window from the right. No face, no other passenger.
- **Layout:** the hand and can fill the lower right half and are cut by the
  bottom edge. The window's bright band runs along the top third.
- **Type:** the headline in the top third, set on the bright, plain window
  band, left-aligned. One supporting line under it at a third of its size:
  the drink's name and its one reason. The logo is on the can; nothing else
  signs it.
- **Palette roles:** paper is the window's light, ink the type, accent unused;
  the can's own colour is the only strong colour in the frame.
- **Why it works:** it is the reader's own view, so the drink is already in
  their hand on their own commute. A product held in a hand from the
  holder's eyes, with no face, is the one static pattern with published
  evidence of higher purchase intent. The product is real, placed first, and
  the scene agrees with it.
- **What to take:** the product shown from the buyer's own point of view, in
  a place they really are.
- **Do not take:** the train, the window band, the condensation, the canned
  drink category.

## 4. A tutoring service — `STICKY_NOTE`, `NO_PACK`

- **Picture:** a generated photograph of a student's desk at night, from 45
  degrees: a maths workbook open under a desk lamp, a pencil across it, an
  eraser worn down to a stub. The workbook's pages are blank (no model is
  asked for writing); the working on them, and one mistake ringed in red, are
  vector icons and text layers in a handwriting face. A blank sticky note,
  generated and cut out in the same lamp light, is stuck to the lamp's base.
- **Layout:** the desk fills the frame and is cropped on three sides. The
  lamp's pool of light is the brightest area, and the note sits in it.
- **Type:** the headline is the note, in the handwriting face: a parent's
  short message to their child, turned and slanted with the note. The
  service's name and its one reason on a single line along the bottom, on
  the dark of the desk. Dates, the address and the price are in the primary
  text, not on the image.
- **Palette roles:** paper is the note, ink the handwriting, accent the red
  ring (the brand's accent), and the desk's dark is the photograph's own.
- **Why it works:** nothing to place, and still a real object with material
  and light, not a flat illustration on a field. The note is something a
  person wrote, so it gets past the reader's ad filter, and the ringed
  mistake is the problem the service removes.
- **What to take:** with no product, the picture is the moment the problem
  happens, made of real things, and the words live on an object in it.
- **Do not take:** the desk, the lamp, the note on the lamp, the red ring.

## 5. A weekend festival — `HEADLINE_PRODUCT`, `NO_PACK`

- **Picture:** a real photograph from last year's edition, which the
  organisers supplied: a crowd from behind, hands up toward a stage, lit
  from the stage. Cropped tight to the hands and the light. No generated
  people: a festival with no photographs of its own would get a BLOCKED and
  a request for them, not a generated crowd.
- **Layout:** the photograph full bleed. The dates are the type picture: two
  numbers set so big that the frame crops their outer edges, across the
  middle, with the raised hands in front of the numbers (the photograph's
  hands cut out and laid above the type).
- **Type:** the numbers in the brand's display face, in paper colour over the
  dark crowd. One supporting line above them: what the festival is and
  where, at a fifth of the numbers' height. The logo bottom left. Tickets,
  the line-up and the address are in the primary text.
- **Palette roles:** paper is the type, ink unused (the photograph is the
  dark), accent a thin rule under the supporting line.
- **Why it works:** a real moment of people enjoying the thing, which is
  what Meta asks for and what a generated crowd would fake. The hands in
  front of the numbers give depth in one move, and the dates read in a
  quarter of a second.
- **What to take:** a real photograph of people at the thing, and type that
  is part of the picture rather than laid over it.
- **Do not take:** the crowd from behind, the cropped dates, the hands over
  the numbers.
